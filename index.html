MONETARY ECONOMICS

Gold & real interest rates

What does a non-yielding asset compete with?

Read more ＋

A real yield measures a bond’s return after allowing for inflation. When real yields fall, the opportunity cost of holding gold can fall too. That is a useful mechanism—not a price forecast. Currency moves, central-bank purchases and uncertainty can pull gold in other directions.

ENERGY & GEOPOLITICS

Oil: pricing the possibility

A supply risk can move prices before a shortage arrives.

Read more ＋

Transport routes and production capacity connect geopolitics to the price of a barrel. Traders may pay a risk premium for a possible disruption, then unwind it when the perceived danger fades. A short-lived rally and a persistent supply shock have very different implications for inflation and growth.

THE BOND MARKET

When the yield curve turns

A steeper curve can tell more than one story.

Read more ＋

An inversion means the selected short-term yield exceeds the long-term yield. Re-steepening can happen as short yields fall on expected easing, or as long yields rise with inflation concerns and term premiums. Those are not interchangeable signals. The curve is a clue to examine alongside jobs, credit and activity—not a recession countdown.

BENEATH THE INDEX

Stable markets, different currents

An index can look calm while its foundations change.

Read more ＋

A handful of large companies can keep a capitalization-weighted index elevated even when participation is narrow. Central-bank assets offer one lens on liquidity, but do not measure every source of market funding. Read balance sheets together with reserves, credit conditions and market breadth.
